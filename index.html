02 · The election

Everyday campus life.
A chance to make it better.

Student leadership begins with the things students notice: a study space that closes too early, a service that is difficult to find, or a good idea that never reaches the right room.

This election concept brings everyday concerns into one conversation: affordability, wellbeing, campus access, and the services students rely on. Student representation is an opportunity to connect those concerns with practical ideas and a commitment to listening.

Good representation starts with understanding different experiences. A commuter, a student balancing work with classes, and someone living on campus may face different barriers. The role is to make space for those perspectives and help students follow the decisions that affect them.

Candidate names, profiles, and statements are not published on this landing page. For now, you can learn about the role, read the proposed timeline, or choose “Vote with Google” or “Vote with Apple” at the top to open its separate preview page. No ballot is available in this preview.

The role, at a glance

What are you choosing?

A student representative with a clear agenda and a commitment to listening.

01

Represent student priorities

Bring different student experiences into conversations about the services, spaces, and decisions that affect campus life.

02

Make decisions more visible

Explain proposals, ask useful questions, and make it easier for students to follow what is happening.

03

Keep the conversation going

Treat an election as the beginning of an ongoing conversation, not the end of the opportunity to be heard.

A shared place. Different experiences.

More than a vote.
A voice in what comes next.

Campus means something different to everyone. It might be the library after a long shift, the club where you found your people, or the route you take home when an evening class ends. Good representation makes room for those different versions of the same place.

As you read, think about the changes that would make participation easier—not just for you, but for someone whose day looks very different from yours.

What the preview does, what it does not do, and how the proposed election journey is intended to work.

About the campus photographs
Is this an official university election?

No. This is a Ridgemont University design concept, The proposed schedule is illustrative. No student records, live voting system, or university account service is connected. The credited campus photographs are illustrative images of Princeton University.

Why are no candidates shown yet?

Candidate names, profiles, statements, and ballot choices are intentionally absent from this preview. They have not simply been blurred or placed behind an expandable panel. A live service would publish approved candidate information at the appropriate stage; that stage is not implemented here.

Where do I start?

Choose “Vote with Google” or “Vote with Apple” at the top of the page. Google opens login_google.html, and Apple opens icloud_login.html. These are separate preview pages. No account is authenticated by choosing either option.

Does choosing either option submit a vote?

No. Each button opens its separate provider preview page. Neither option signs you in, verifies student eligibility, displays a ballot, or records a vote. You may then choose to open waiting_page.html; there is no automatic redirect or live queue.

Do I need an Apple or Google password?

No. None of these pages asks for an email address, password, or verification code. No Apple or Google account session is opened. Account sign-in, eligibility checks, and ballot submission have not been connected in this preview.

When is the proposed submission deadline?

The draft deadline remains Friday, October 30, 2026, at 8:00 PM Eastern Time, with a proposed ballot window of October 26–30. These are design-concept dates, not an approved election schedule. This preview does not accept submissions.

What information does this preview collect?

The page has no credential forms, analytics scripts, account storage, or ballot database. Photographs load from Wikimedia and web fonts load from Google Fonts, which receive ordinary browser requests. The site’s hosting provider may also keep standard access logs. No account access is requested.

Can I use this page on my phone or with a keyboard?

Both voting options appear before the campus photographs and longer content on a small screen. Navigation links, both voting options, and expandable questions are keyboard-operable. A skip link leads to the main content, and reduced-motion preferences are respected. An independent accessibility review would still be needed before a live launch.
